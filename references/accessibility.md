# Stop Slop: Accessibility Guardrails

Visual restraint must not reduce usability.

## Structure
- Use semantic headings, navigation, main content, lists, tables, forms, and buttons.
- Maintain a logical heading hierarchy.
- Give inputs persistent, programmatically associated labels.
- Give icon-only controls accessible names.
- Use buttons for actions and links for navigation.

## Keyboard and interaction
- Essential actions must be keyboard accessible.
- Focus order must follow the meaningful reading and action order.
- Keep visible focus indicators.
- Do not make hover the only way to reveal information.
- Provide clear disabled, loading, success, and error states.
- Do not trap keyboard users in menus or dialogs.

## Contrast and readability
- Keep text readable against backgrounds in every state.
- Do not use low opacity or pale colours as the only way to communicate important content.
- Do not communicate status through colour alone.
- Preserve readable line lengths and font sizes.

## Responsive behaviour
- Support narrow screens without ordinary content overflowing horizontally.
- Reflow complex layouts instead of merely shrinking desktop grids.
- Keep touch targets practical and separated.
- Do not hide critical mobile content without a usable alternative.

## Motion and media
- Respect reduced-motion preferences.
- Do not make essential information depend on animation.
- Avoid flashing or continuously moving decoration.
- Provide text alternatives for meaningful images.

## Audit
Can keyboard-only users complete the main task? Is focus visible? Are controls labelled? Are errors understandable? Is text legible at normal zoom? Does the interface work with reduced motion?
