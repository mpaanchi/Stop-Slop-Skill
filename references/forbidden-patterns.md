# Stop Slop: Forbidden Pattern Reference

These patterns become slop when applied automatically, repeatedly, or without a user-centred reason. They are not universal design laws.

## Interface furniture
- Decorative tags, chips, pills, badges, and eyebrow labels.
- Decorative section numbers and fake code breadcrumbs.
- Borders and containers around every block.
- Dividers between every section or list item.
- Icons beside every heading or bullet.
- Squircles and exaggerated radii applied everywhere.
- ASCII art, dithering, grain, and noise used as filler.

## Typography and copy
- Instrument Serif as an automatic premium font.
- Decorative kerning, exaggerated tracking, and excessive italics.
- Monospace outside code or technical values.
- ALL-CAPS subtitles and shouty microcopy.
- Periods at the end of short headings without a grammatical reason.
- Multiple low-contrast text shades and opacity levels without semantic meaning.
- Verbose filler, repeated claims, vague adjectives, and self-congratulatory copy.

## Layout
- The default centred hero, two CTAs, three feature cards, and repeated CTA sequence.
- Bento grids used simply because they are fashionable.
- Turning every paragraph, list, statistic, or feature into a card.
- Centre-aligning all content regardless of reading flow.
- Empty space that inflates the page or hides useful content.
- Over-designed dashboards, redundant KPI rows, filters, panels, and decorative charts.
- Elaborate diagrams when text or a simple diagram is clearer.
- Multiple equally prominent CTAs competing for attention.

## Colour and effects
- Generic blue-purple gradients, gradient text, rainbow accents, and gradient backgrounds.
- Glassmorphism, heavy backdrop blur, neon glow, and glowing borders without a purpose.
- Shadows on every surface.
- Accent borders repeated on every card, quote, and callout.
- Too many unrelated corner radii.

## Motion and imagery
- Fade-ins on every section, floating elements, animated gradients, and decorative pulses.
- Scroll-jacking, unnecessary parallax, and delayed reveals.
- Hover scaling that adds no useful feedback.
- Generic office stock photography and irrelevant lifestyle imagery.
- Abstract 3D blobs, cubes, and glass spheres used instead of relevant product visuals.
- Emoji replacing a consistent icon system.

## Starter-kit tells
- Untouched shadcn/Radix components presented as finished art direction.
- Repeated Tailwind combinations of rounded corners, borders, shadows, and padding on nearly every block.
- Dark navy + electric blue/purple gradient + glass cards + glow as an automatic AI-product identity.
- Oversized headline + tiny uppercase eyebrow + muted paragraph + paired CTA as the automatic hero.
- Repeated three-column feature cards with identical icon/title/description structure.

## Exception test
Before keeping a discouraged pattern, finish this sentence: "This element is here because it helps the user ______." If the answer is only "it looks modern", "it fills space", or "the template had it", remove it.

Legitimate filters, status labels, breadcrumbs, borders, charts, animation, and brand treatments should remain when they serve a real purpose.
